## Highlights

5 PRs merged, 1 other opened and 63 commits across two repos, with no
releases. Two issues were opened: the Aiken bump (#2893), closed by its
merge, and a migration away from the deprecated cardano-api transaction
body API (#2896), left open. The merges raise the snapshot transaction
cap, correct some benchmark measurements, tighten deposit observation and
decommit handling, and update the Aiken toolchain. The one other PR, a
cardano-node and cardano-api bump, was still in review at the week's end.

**Reactive.** The snapshot cap rose from 1000 to 4000 transactions (#2856,
merged Tuesday, open since 4 September). Each snapshot round does work
over the pending backlog, so a small cap makes a heavily loaded head
repeat that work more often. In the recorded local three-node benchmark,
draining 24000 self-transfers took 13.50 seconds at the new cap against
37.04 at the old one: effective throughput rose from 648 to 1778 tx/s,
and median confirmation time fell from 29.1 to 12.3 seconds. These are
burst-drain measurements with minimal transactions and loopback etcd;
the cap does not bind under light load. Larger caps still helped in the
sweep, but gains tapered while request size grew, so 4000 leaves ample
room below etcd's request limit. The cap limits what a leader proposes;
followers already accept larger requests, and the separate limit on the
head's UTxO size is unchanged.

The PR benchmark reports were tightened Friday (#2898). The sustained-TPS
slope now needs eight snapshot points inside its measurement window,
up from four, so a run with only a handful of large snapshots no longer
gets a misleading slope. A comparison row is coloured only when at least
three quarters of its pairs measured the metric, as well as meeting the
existing noise and direction checks. The memory row now reports mean
live heap over major garbage collections during transaction processing,
taking the largest node's mean, instead of a lifetime peak that small
scenarios could set during startup. The metric is omitted if no node had
a major collection in that window; monitoring and benchmark tests and
documentation were updated alongside it.

Two protocol follow-ups also merged Friday (#2899). A fanout whose first
output looked like a deposit, and whose transaction had an upper validity
bound, could be observed as a deposit instead: the deposit observer matched
the output's shape before the fanout observer ran, leaving the nodes
closed even though the fanout had landed. Head transactions are now
checked first, and any transaction spending a head output is excluded
from deposit observation. Regression coverage includes full and final
partial fanouts.

The same PR tightened the pending-decommit case from last week's #2891.
A snapshot request at the same version must keep the decommit the
confirmed snapshot is still settling. Dropping it, or substituting a
deposit, could leave the honest nodes without a snapshot they could use
to close and fan out once the decrement landed. Such requests are now
rejected with `ReqSnDecommitNotSettled`. Side-loading a snapshot also keeps
the matching decommit if it is still settling, instead of pruning it with
the unconfirmed transactions. The protocol documentation, Agda reference
and tests were updated to match.

Aiken moved from 1.1.9 to 1.1.24 and its standard library from 2.1.0 to
4.0.0 (#2894, merged Tuesday, closes #2893). The validator utility was
adapted to the library's `Assets` type, the compiled blueprint regenerated,
and the cached standard library used by the Nix tests updated. A separate
tidy that day (#2895) removed Nix deprecation warnings from `just check`
by replacing uses of `system` with `stdenv.hostPlatform.system`.

The next dependency upgrade, to cardano-node 11.1.3 and cardano-api 11.7,
opened Thursday (#2897). Migrating the deprecated transaction body API to
`Cardano.Api.Experimental` is tracked separately in #2896, keeping that
larger redesign out of the version bump.

**KPI & Outcome Achievement (M6).** The W40 note published, the tracker
recorded the workshop's completion of Ecosystem Support, and the delivery
timeline was tidied. Shipped dates now have their own markers and tooltips,
distinct from the deadlines they met.
